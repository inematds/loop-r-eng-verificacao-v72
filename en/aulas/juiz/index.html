Lesson 6 · 22 min

Model judge with a calibrated rubric

Not everything becomes a test: "does the message explain why?" takes reading. A model can judge that, as long as the rubric is yes/no, the judgment is repeated, and the judge has been compared with your labels before it counts. In this lesson you calibrate a fake judge (which fails), then a real one, and you see the calibration catch an ambiguous rubric.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Yes/no rubric, an invariable criterion, and what stays outside it
  2. 2Three repetitions, shuffled order, majority wins
  3. 3Agreement and kappa: why 80% may not be enough
  4. 4A judge with no answer is not a judge that failed it

Goal: write a yes/no rubric, run a judge with repetitions, and only use it after it is calibrated against human labels.

02 · The rehearsal rubric

Commit messages written by an agent. 10 messages in kit/juiz/artefatos.jsonl, with one person's labels in humano.csv.

kit/juiz/rubrica.md (translation for reading; the file in the kit is in Portuguese)
# Rubric — commit message written by the agent

Yes/no criteria. Never a 1-to-10 score. (invariável) = a "nao" fails the message, regardless of the rest.
What can be checked by code (length of the 1st line, format) does NOT go here: it goes to a test.

R1 | Does the message say WHY the change was made, not just what changed? | qualidade
R2 | Does every CHANGE the message mentions appear in the diff (it doesn't invent a file, test or effect)? The reason may come from outside the diff. | invariável
R3 | Can someone who hasn't seen the diff understand what changed from the message alone? | qualidade
Terminal · rehearsal with the fake judge
J=~/projetos/loop-r-eng-verificacao-v72/kit/juiz
python3 $J/juiz.py --rep 3 --saida /tmp/juiz-falso.csv
python3 $J/calibrar.py $J/humano.csv /tmp/juiz-falso.csv; echo "saída: $?"
Example from the repository
a01  R1=sim R2=sim R3=sim
a02  R1=nao R2=sim R3=nao
a03  R1=sim R2=sim R3=sim
(… a04 a a10 …)
→ /tmp/juiz-falso.csv
10 artefatos rotulados pelos dois
R1  concordância 80%  kappa 0.55  REESCREVA ou troque o juiz  (discorda em a03 a09)
R2  concordância 70%  kappa 0.00  REESCREVA ou troque o juiz  (discorda em a03 a05 a09)
R3  concordância 100%  kappa 1.00  ok
JUIZ DESCALIBRADO em R1, R2: não use para aprovar
saída: 1

The fake judge says "sim" on R2 for everything: it never checks the diff. The calibration caught it. Notice R1: 80% agreement looks like a pass, but the kappa (0.55) shows that a good part of it is chance.

If almost everything is "yes", a judge that always answers "yes" already agrees with you a lot. Cohen's kappa discounts that agreement expected by chance: 1 is perfect, 0 is the same as guessing. FEC asks for 0.6 with 30 labeled samples; LOOP-R asks for 80% agreement. calibrar.py enforces both.

03 · The real judge and what the calibration found

Uses your subscription quota: 30 calls (10 messages × 3). On 10/19/2026 this ran twice; the first run showed a flaw in the rubric, not in the judge.

Terminal · real judge (continues from the previous block)
JUIZ_CMD='claude -p --tools ""' python3 $J/juiz.py --rep 3 --saida /tmp/juiz-real.csv
python3 $J/calibrar.py $J/humano.csv /tmp/juiz-real.csv; echo "saída: $?"
Example from the repository
a01  R1=sim R2=sim R3=sim
a02  R1=nao R2=sim R3=nao
a03  R1=nao R2=nao R3=sim  instável: R1 2×1, R3 2×1  REPROVA (R2 invariável)
a04  R1=sim R2=sim R3=sim
a05  R1=sim R2=sim R3=sim
(… a06 a a08 …)
a09  R1=nao R2=nao R3=sim  instável: R1 2×1  REPROVA (R2 invariável)
a10  R1=sim R2=sim R3=sim
→ /tmp/juiz-real.csv
10 artefatos rotulados pelos dois
R1  concordância 100%  kappa 1.00  ok
R2  concordância 90%  kappa 0.74  ok  (discorda em a05)
R3  concordância 100%  kappa 1.00  ok
JUIZ CALIBRADO
saída: 0

Claude Code 2.1.303, the subscription's default model, 3 min 12 s. The divergence that remained: a05 says that increasing the session "improves security"; the person marked it as an invented effect, the judge accepted it.

In the first real round, R2 said "Tudo o que a mensagem afirma aparece no diff (nada inventado)?". The judge failed good messages, because the REASON for a change is never in the diff:

a01  R1=sim R2=nao R3=sim  REPROVA (R2 invariável)
a04  R1=sim R2=nao R3=sim  REPROVA (R2 invariável)

The judge was right by the letter; the rubric was the ambiguous one. The criterion was rewritten ("every CHANGE cited is in the diff; the reason may come from outside") and the second round calibrated. That is what calibration is for: finding the bad criterion before it decides anything.

In that same first round, the subscription hit the session limit from the 6th artifact on. The old version of juiz.py counted the missing answer as "nao" and failed everything. The current version separates them: it prints SEM RESPOSTA do juiz, leaves the artifact out of the CSV and ends with JUIZ FALHOU em N chamada(s) and a nonzero exit code. A judge that did not answer neither failed nor passed.

06 · In three sentences

Binary rubricYes-or-no questions, invariants that eliminate; whatever code can check goes into a test.
Repeat and calibrateThree repetitions with shuffled order and, before using it, 80% agreement and kappa of 0.6 against your own labels.
Calibration finds a bad rubricDivergence may come from the criterion, not the judge; and a judge with no answer is an error, not a failure.
